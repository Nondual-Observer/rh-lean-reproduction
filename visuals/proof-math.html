RH is a statement about how evenly primes are spread

Weight each prime power pᵏ by log p and add up to x. That gives Chebyshev's function ψ(x). The prime number theorem says ψ(x) ≈ x. The Riemann Hypothesis says how close: the error never grows faster than about √x.

RH ⇒ |ψ(x) − x| < (1/8π) · √x · (log x)² for every x ≥ 73.2

That precise form is Schoenfeld's (1976), and Harald Helfgott raised it on the Lean Zulip as a check anyone can read. Conversely, any bound of this √x shape implies RH (plate 3). Drag the slider out to a million: the error, in teal, comes within about 8% of the band but never crosses it.

The error is a sum of waves, one per zero of ζ

Riemann's explicit formula writes the prime staircase exactly as x minus one wave for each nontrivial zero ρ = β + iγ of ζ:

ψ(x) = x − Σρ xρ/ρ − log 2π − ½ log(1 − x−2)

A zero at height γ contributes a wave of frequency γ in log x and size about xβ/|ρ|. If every β is ½ (that is RH), every wave grows like √x. Add zeros with the slider and watch the smooth curve snap onto the staircase. The zeros themselves are where Hardy's Z(t) crosses zero on the critical line (top plot).

What if a zero were off the line?

Move the first zero to real part β. Its wave grows like xβ, and for any β > ½ it eventually outgrows Schoenfeld's band from plate 1. That is why RH and the square-root bound are the same statement.

A bound on prime sums clears a half-plane of zeros

The logic also runs backwards, and this is the direction the proof uses. If the prime sums are controlled with exponent θ, then the integral ∫ (ψ(x) − x) x−s−1 dx converges for Re s > θ. That integral equals −ζ′(s)/(s ζ(s)) − 1/(s − 1), so ζ can have no zeros there.

|ψ(x) − x| ≤ C·xθ+ε ⇒ ζ(s) ≠ 0 for Re s > θ

Slide θ down to ½: the cleared half-plane meets the critical line and RH follows by symmetry. The same holds for each Dirichlet L(s, χ) with ψ(x, χ) = Σ χ(n)Λ(n). Doing it for every χ at once is exactly the statement EveryCharacter at the top of the Lean proof.

Because Λ(n) ≥ 0, Landau's theorem on Dirichlet series with nonnegative coefficients means a one-sided bound already suffices. The community reading of the proof says its final step is a "windowed" version of this Landau argument.

Over finite fields, RH is a theorem

Count the points of an elliptic curve y² = x³ + ax + b modulo a prime p. You expect about p + 1. Hasse proved the error ap = p + 1 − #E(𝔽p) obeys |ap| ≤ 2√p. That is the Riemann Hypothesis for this curve's zeta function, and Weil extended it to all curves.

Below, every dot is one prime, computed live. Its height is ap/(2√p), which is the error measured against √p. Try any curve: the dots never leave the band. This is the "toy setting" Alpöge's note starts from.

Stepanov's trick: vanishing to high order buys a square root

Stepanov (1969) gave an elementary proof of these finite-field bounds. He builds an auxiliary polynomial that vanishes at every point being counted, and to order m at each, so each point "uses up" m of the polynomial's degree. A nonzero polynomial of degree D has at most D/m such roots, so the count is bounded.

Raising m sharpens the count, but building the polynomial gets harder as m grows. The two costs pull in opposite directions, roughly q/m and m, and they balance at m ≈ √q. That is where the square root in |N − q| ≲ √q comes from.

The note's idea: multiplicity over the integers, one theta lift at a time

Over the integers there is no "vanishing to order m": the note likens it to differentiating whole numbers. Its proposal is to get multiplicity from automorphic forms instead. A chain of theta lifts between definite unitary groups U(X₀) → U(X₁) → ⋯ → U(Xm) adds one unit of multiplicity per lift.

The note says that for a von Mangoldt sum of length X, the right chain length is √X, adding "And yet, what else could it be?" It is Stepanov's balance again, with X in place of q. Slide X and the chain re-forms at its best length.

The note also says that if each lift's cost is paid separately the method stops at exponent 2/3. Jonas Whidden's reading says the fix is to track divisibility along the whole chain inside one integral lattice, so the costs do not simply add up.

Not verified. Nobody has yet written out this argument in a form mathematicians have checked. The Lean code is checked; this plate is only the shape of the idea as the note and one reader describe it.
