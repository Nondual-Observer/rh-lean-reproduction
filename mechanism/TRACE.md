Ishaan Dixit, 20 November 2026. Working notes; each claim is labelled.

## Goal

Alpöge's note says a chain of about √X theta lifts plays the role of multiplicity in Stepanov's method, and that charged lift by lift the method stalls at exponent 2/3. Nobody has explained how the code realises this. These notes work from the top theorem downward and record what each layer actually states.

**Why a one-sided bound is enough (my reading, checked by arithmetic).** With o − 1 nontrivial characters weighted 1/(3o+1) each, every Dirichlet coefficient of the combined series is at least Λ(n)·(1 − (o−1)/(3o+1)) > 0. Positive coefficients are exactly what Landau's theorem needs, so an upper bound on Gm alone forces zero-freeness. This matches the community description of a "windowed Landau argument".

The exact list shows that no declaration in the namespace `Formalism.HC.Landau` is used. That file proves a Landau theorem the final proof does not use; the step the proof uses is the windowed `lemmaL` above.

**Link to the note.** Gz is, symbol for symbol, the expression in step 5 of the note's timeline ("definitively switch to Λ"): Σ_{n≤X} Λ(n)(X/n − 1) − X log X + (1+γ)X. Step 5 therefore lives in `Formalism/StatementOC.lean`, under no name a search for "Guinand–Weil" would find. Verified.

## 2. The √X chain length is a hypothesis of the bound

`core_gm` needs `CoreAtH`, defined in `Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/PrimitiveCosets/CoreAtH.lean:29`. It packages a record `Dd : Data U0 W Top m f` plus size and window conditions. One of them is `RestSize`, `Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/SChain.lean:53`, which includes

```
x ^ (1/2 − ε) ≤ m        and        k ≤ n² · x ^ (1/2 − ε)
```

so the parameter `m` must be at least about √x. Verified. That `m` is the length of the chain of theta lifts is **Reading**: in `Data` (`Formalism/RankGapBounds/MeanBoundSecondForm.lean`), `m` indexes per-step weights `w` and energies `EeJ`, and the energy is their sum over j < m, which fits one term per lift, but the record itself is just real numbers.

The same block requires a "move cost" of at most x^(1/2 + ε) (`XLarge`, same file). Verified.

## 3. The layers so far

1. **Endgame** (verified): square-root bound on Gm ⇒ Landau ⇒ no zeros ⇒ `EveryCharacter` ⇒ RH.
2. **Inequality layer** (verified as a shape): `Data` is a bookkeeping record of real numbers. `SizeData₂` (`SChain.lean`) bundles its hypotheses: `HidaSetting`, `DIV`, `EComp5`, `HComp`, `Count5`, `Slopes`, `QuotientSlopeBoundsWith`, `LossBounds`, `SlopeIneq`, `HHBound`, `FParams`. Lemmas such as `Ga_bound5` turn these into bounds. No automorphic objects appear here.
3. **Tie to arithmetic** (located, not yet read): `ChainTied`, `Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/TieGlobal.lean:97`, attaches the abstract `Data` to a family `FamP` of arithmetic objects through `TiePts`, `TieGlobal`, `TieLocal` and `TieArith`. The family's host carries two primes `q` and `d`, matching the `WinPair` conditions (q ≡ 3 mod 8, d ≡ 7 mod 16) in the window bound. This is where theta lifts, Hida theory and divisibility should enter.

## 5. Where √X comes from: the balance, verified line by line

The whole square-root saving is one inequality, `proposition_size_bound` (`Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/ChainSlopeWindows/LipschitzWeightAndMinimum.lean:507`, Exact: used). It proves `GrowthSizeBound`:

```
nT·m·b·(δ₂/2)·(G(x) − 4x^(1/2+ε))  ≤  Err + 2·nT·m·b/x        (main term on the left)
Err ≤ x^(Cε)·[ nT·aE·(1 + m²/k)  +  m·(2n·m·b·x^ε)  +  m·(nT2·k·b·x^ε / (n²·x^(−ε))) ]
⟹  G(x) ≤ x^(1/2 + (C+3)ε)
```

Here G = `Gz`, the ζ-part of the explicit formula (section 1), m is the chain length, n = |H| the size of the character group, nT and nT2 are counts of "top" and "thick" points, b and aE ≈ x·b are scales, and k is a second size parameter. The constraints come from `SizeWindows` / `RestSize` (same files):

| Constraint | Used for |
|---|---|
| aE ≤ 2xb | |
| m ≥ x^(1/2 − ε) | term 1 |
| m ≤ x^(1/2 + ε/2) | term 3 |
| x·m ≤ k ≤ n²·x^(1/2 − ε) | terms 2 and 4 |
| nT ≥ x^(−ε/8)·n/2, nT2/n ≥ x^(−ε) | dividing out |

Dividing each error term by the main-term size nT·m·b gives four lemmas in the same file (`SZ.T1`–`SZ.T4`, verified):

| Term | After dividing by nT·m·b | Bound | Lemma |
|---|---|---|---|
| nT·aE | aE/(m·b) ≈ **x/m** | ≤ x^(1/2+ε), needs m ≥ √x | `T1` |
| nT·aE·m²/k | x·m/k | ≤ 2, needs k ≥ x·m | `T2` |
| m·(2n·m·b·x^ε) | 2n·m/nT ≈ **m** | ≤ x^(1/2+…), needs m ≤ √x | `T3` |
| m·(nT2·k·b·…) | k/(n·nT) | ≤ x^(1/2+…), needs k ≤ n²√x | `T4` |

So the error, relative to the main term, is about **x/m + m**, and both are √x exactly when m ≍ √x. The lower bound m ≥ x^(1/2−ε) and upper bound m ≤ x^(1/2+ε/2) pin the chain length to √x from both sides. Verified.

**Where 2/3 would come from (Reading, checked by arithmetic).** The m-term is m·B0 with B0 ≤ 2n·m·b·x^ε (`BNBounds`, `Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/SChain.lean:34`, Exact: used). So the total chain cost is quadratic in m, and per unit of main term it is linear in m. If that cost were one power of m worse (cubic total, for example each of the m lifts paying an O(m²) integrality cost on its own), the balance would be x/m + m², minimised at m = x^(1/3) with value x^(2/3). That matches the outline's statement that charging lifts separately stalls at 2/3. The code does not mention 2/3 anywhere on the proof path; this is my reconstruction of the counterfactual.

**Correction (checked after writing the line above).** B0 is not a free quantity that must be shown small. It is *defined* as a budget: `SizeData₂.B0 := sizeBudget D.R x ε = f·m·b·x^ε` (`Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataParams₂.lean`, with `sizeBudget` in `DataArith.lean:200`; f = `R.f`, b = exp(x^ε)). So `BNBounds` is bookkeeping (it should reduce to f ≤ 2|H|; not yet checked). The content is in the statements that real quantities **fit inside** this budget, chiefly the per-lift energy condition `EComp` (section 6), which the family proof gets from `LinkDIV_at` (`Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalTheoremInputs.lean`):

```
for every word u and every lift j = 1..m:
  | Σ_w sz(w, E(u,j,w))  −  (Σ_t aT t)·log(g₀·g₁) |  ≤  C·B0 = C·f·m·b·x^ε
```

The budget per lift grows linearly in m. Summed over m lifts that is the m² total cost of section 5.

## 6. The slope layer: where the "same slope cancels"

The per-point bound used by the proof is `each_unit_bound_statement` (`…/ChainSlopeWindows/LevelOfCongruence.lean:175`, Exact: used), which proves `PointwiseBoundVariant`: ASK(u0) ≤ Main(u0) + `ErrX5at`(u0). (The averaged version `MeanBoundWith`/`errorTermWith` exists in the source but is **not** used; Exact.) Its hypotheses, bundled as `SizeData₂`:

- `EComp5`: |Ee(u0,u) − m·sumA·lg| ≤ cE·m·B0. The chain energy Ee is m copies of one step's slope (sumA·lg, with lg = log(g₀g₁) from the host, `TieGlobal`), up to error m·B0. This is where "tracking divisibility along the chain" has to land: the total energy is linear in m with an error controlled by B0.
- `QuotientSlopeBoundsWith`: the maximal slope of the quotient lattice N is at most that of M, plus gain Ga and Fo, minus Ee, plus cSl1·m·B0; and a matching lower bound. These are Bost-style slope inequalities: `muMaxN`, `muMaxM`, `muN`, `muM` are tied by `TieLocal` to `(Fm.D x u0).div.Nu u` (rank, μ, μ_max) and `((Fm.D x u0).fm u).lat 0`.
- `Count5`, `Slopes`, `DIV`, `HComp`, `LossBounds`, `SlopeIneq`, `HHBound`, `FParams`: counting, divisibility and loss bookkeeping.

The cancellation Whidden described is visible: `Ga_bound5` (line 113) combines `s_lower5`, which uses the **upper** bound Ee ≤ m(sumA·lg + cE·B0), with `key_identity5` (line 105), which contains **+m·sumA·lg**. The m·sumA·lg terms cancel, leaving only the m·cE·B0 error. The proof of `Ga_bound5` ends in `linarith`. Verified. As he said, the cancellation is routine; the content is in proving `EComp5` and `QuotientSlopeBoundsWith` for the real family with B0 this small.

## 7. Who supplies the hypotheses

`ChainTied` (`…/GlobalSchurBounds/TieGlobal.lean:97`) is the bridge. It asserts that an abstract `Data` record exists whose fields equal the arithmetic objects of the family (`TiePts`, `TieGlobal`, `TieLocal`, `TieArith`), and that `SizeData₂`, `ErrWindows`, `BNBounds`, `RestSize` and the rest hold for it. In particular `TieLocal` sets `Dd.EeJ u0 u j = Σ_w sz(w, E(u, j+1, w))`: the energy of step j of the chain, summed over places w. Verified.

`ChainTied` is produced by `linkCore` (`…/GlobalSchurBounds/LossesSmallShares.lean:38`, Exact: used) from fourteen family-level hypotheses: `CountF`, `SlopesMF`, `SlopesNF`, `LatF`, `NodesF`, `FrameF`, `LossFS`, `CharF`, `WinF`, `ChosenTT`, `ChosenQD`, `ChosenThin`, `LinkIRR`, `LinkDIV`.

Two theorems call `linkCore`. Only one of them is used:

So the named "Kudla family statements" and `TaylorLogMetric` inputs of `linkGLOBAL7_pk` are not on the proof path. The used route, `linkGLOBAL7_items`, takes these inputs instead:
